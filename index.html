Why it feels like a real lab

Four things separate a demonstration from an experiment. All four are built in.

/ 01

Real apparatus

Radium source, lead collimator, gold foil and a fluorescent screen — laid out the way they sat on the bench in 1909, so the setup itself teaches you something.

/ 02

Real physics, not playback

Every alpha particle is integrated through the Coulomb force in real time. The scattering angles you see are computed, not drawn.

/ 03

Live data

Counters, an angle-resolved detector and a filling histogram turn the show into measurements you can actually read off.

/ 04

Guided observation

An observation log records the remarkable events as they happen, then hands you the conclusion Rutherford had to reach.

Start with the experiment that redrew the atom

In 1909, Geiger and Marsden fired alpha particles at gold foil expecting them to sail through. About one in every few thousand came straight back. Rutherford said it was "almost as incredible as if you fired a 15-inch shell at a piece of tissue paper and it came back and hit you."

Open the lab and see it for yourself — the beam, the flashes, the numbers and the conclusion.
