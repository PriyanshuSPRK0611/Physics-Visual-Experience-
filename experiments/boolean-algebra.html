/ 01

Two values, three operations

Every variable is 0 or 1. Everything else is built from NOT, AND and OR — the other gates are just convenient combinations of them.

AND · OR + NOT ¬
/ 02

A truth table is the definition

Two expressions are equal exactly when their truth tables match row for row. That is how every law on this page is proved.

/ 03

De Morgan's theorems

The two most useful identities in all of digital design — they let you swap AND for OR by moving the bar.

¬(A·B) = ¬A + ¬B
¬(A+B) = ¬A · ¬B
/ 04

Why simplify?

Each term is a logic gate. A simpler expression is fewer gates, less power and a faster circuit. The Karnaugh map and Quine–McCluskey method find the smallest form.

From logic to silicon

Every processor, every memory chip and every microcontroller is, at the bottom, a vast pile of Boolean expressions built from NOT, AND and OR. When you simplify an expression on this board, you are doing by hand the same job a synthesis tool does when it turns a line of code into transistors.

Flip the switches, watch the truth tables, and get a feel for how two values can be arranged into logic — and how the right rearrangement makes it cheaper.
